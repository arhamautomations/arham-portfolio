Early Days
AGE 3
LANDMINE ROBOT EXPO

Long before robotics became a degree or a career path, curiosity was already taking over. At just three years old, Arham was more interested in pulling apart multimeters and soldering irons than playing with toys. As he grew older, that curiosity became a fascination with machines that seemed almost alive. He wanted to understand how robotic arms could move with such precision, how humanoid robots could perceive their surroundings and move autonomously without a remote controller, and what made a machine capable of making decisions on its own. Those questions became the kind of spark that would not let him sleep. That curiosity soon turned into building. He experimented with mechanisms, built walking robots, and eventually designed a landmine detection robot for a school technology expo. The robot became an eye catcher at the exhibition, drawing the attention of a retired colonel who stopped to take a closer look at the young maker and his creation. Moments like that made the questions feel real: robotics was no longer just something fascinating to watch, but something he wanted to understand, build, and dedicate his path to.

Now

I'm an M.S. candidate in Autonomous Systems and Robotics at Illinois Institute of Technology (Dec 2026), based in Chicago and open to opportunities. My research and development interest centers on industrial robotics and automation, the specific problems I keep coming back to:

Robot Arms & Motion Control

I'm especially drawn to collaborative robots and the idea of teaching an arm by physically guiding it through a motion rather than coding every waypoint by hand. That interest carries into multi axis closed loop motion systems, where forward and inverse kinematics, path and trajectory planning, and motor synchronization algorithms all have to work together, alongside techniques like Bresenham inspired interpolation and swept volume collision detection to keep a coordinated move both smooth and safe. I am also working on a vision based guidance system for teaching robotic arms through a camera and pen drawn paths, allowing a user to draw a desired motion and have the robot learn and reproduce that trajectory.

Industrial Automation & Controls

On the automation side, my focus is on the systems that actually run a factory floor: PLC programming in ladder logic and structured text, designing HMIs an operator can trust at a glance, integrating sensors and actuators into pneumatic and hydraulic systems, and building special purpose machines, like rotary indexing stations, for specific production tasks.

Autonomous Systems & Vision

I'm also building out the autonomy side of things: ROS based system architecture, LiDAR and camera based perception, IMU and encoder sensing, sensor fusion, localization and mapping (SLAM), real time pose estimation, path planning, obstacle avoidance, and vision guided robotics using OpenCV. I'm especially interested in closed loop autonomous systems that can perceive their environment, estimate their state, plan a safe motion, and continuously correct their movement instead of relying on open loop commands.

U01 · PROJECT / 01
Vision Based Virtual Path Teaching for Robot Arms

This project implements a Programming by Demonstration system that lets a user simply draw a path on paper with a pen, then teaches that same path to a robotic arm. The system uses a camera together with YOLO based marker tracking to follow the pen tip in real time and estimate its pose as it moves. Captured points are cleaned up using noise filtering and path simplification so that small hand tremors do not translate into jerky robot motion. A shape recognition stage identifies common geometric patterns in the drawing and converts them into smooth, robot ready trajectories that can be executed directly by the arm controller, removing the need for manual coordinate programming.

U02 · PROJECT / 02
Collision Aware Motion Planning for Robotic Arms

A full simulator built for a two degree of freedom robotic arm that models both forward and inverse kinematics, so a target end effector position can be converted into the required joint angles. The simulator generates smooth trajectories directly in joint space and renders the arm motion in real time, so behavior can be checked visually before running on real hardware. Because the inverse kinematics equations often produce more than one valid solution, the system includes logic for choosing the correct inverse kinematics branch. It also performs swept volume collision detection along the whole motion path, and supports waypoint based path planning so that multiple intermediate targets can be chained together into a single continuous move.

U03 · PROJECT / 03
Multi Axis Force Sensor Design for Robotic Control

Design and analysis of a three axis force sensor built around a cross flexure mechanism, which allows small, measurable deflections under load while remaining mechanically robust. Virtual strain gauges are placed at key points on the flexure to translate those deflections into readable signals. The design work includes full stiffness and strain analysis of the flexure geometry, along with a Wheatstone bridge circuit analysis used to condition and amplify the sensor output. A companion MATLAB simulator visualizes the sensed forces in real time and models expected sensor noise, helping validate the design before physical prototyping.

U04 · PROJECT / 04
CARIS: Cartesian Robot Intelligent System

CARIS is a Cartesian robot built to deliver precise, synchronized motion across multiple axes using real time feedback and closed loop control. Rather than requiring users to write code, the robot is programmed through a lead through teaching method, where an operator simply guides the tool using a joystick and a handful of switches, and the system records the resulting path for later playback. This approach was designed to make the robot accessible to non programmers on a factory floor. The platform has been applied to pick and place operations, precision assembly, welding, and spray painting tasks, and the work was published as research in the INDICA Journal.

U05 · PROJECT / 05
Low Cost Digital Volt Ammeter

An affordable, heavy duty digital volt ammeter designed for industrial environments, built to withstand the electrical noise and physical wear typical of factory settings. The device includes IoT connectivity so readings can be monitored remotely without needing someone physically present at the panel. Compared to similar meters available on the market, this design uses fewer components, simplifying manufacturing and reducing potential points of failure, while onboard filtering algorithms suppress electrical disturbance so readings stay accurate even in noisy industrial power environments. The meter was launched commercially and tested directly on real production machines.

U06 · PROJECT / 06
Smart Hopper Level Controller

An autonomous monitoring and control system for industrial hoppers that uses non contact ultrasonic sensing to continuously track fluid levels without any part of the sensor touching the liquid. Based on preset high and low limits, the system automatically switches connected pumps on and off, preventing both overflow at the top and dry run conditions at the bottom that can damage pump hardware. The underlying control logic was designed to be flexible enough to adapt to both large industrial hoppers and smaller domestic water tanks with minimal reconfiguration.

Ultrasonic SensingAutomation
U07 · PROJECT / 07
MIG Welder Machine

Design and build of a complete MIG welding machine that integrates several subsystems into one working unit. A three phase current transformer supplies and regulates the welding current, while a DC motor driven wire feeder, fitted with potentiometer feedback for closed loop speed control, delivers filler wire at a consistent rate. Shielding gas is managed through a solenoid valve controlled CO2 release system that times gas flow to match the weld cycle. All of these subsystems are tied together through a central control circuit, with front panel selector switches that let an operator quickly adjust current and weld settings to match the thickness of the job being welded.

Power ElectronicsMotor Control
U08 · PROJECT / 08
Welding Automatic Data Logging System

An Arduino based data logging system built to capture key welding parameters, such as current and wire feed speed, during every weld cycle and store them onto an SD card in a structured, traceable record. Welders and process engineers can later review these logs to analyze performance across many welds, track how consistent the process has been over time, and identify specific parameters that may be affecting overall weld quality, supporting continuous process improvement on the shop floor.

Vision
I want to eliminate the myth that robots displace human labor and take away people's jobs.

I'm passionate about building collaborative robots and flexible automation systems that can work alongside humans safely and reliably, at a low enough cost that even small scale industries can afford them.

These systems should be so easy to program that even a kid could teach or program one, and they should be convenient enough to integrate with any existing control systems in an industry, without needing complexity or a separate, large team just to make it work.

I also aspire to work with autonomous systems: warehouse robots, delivery robots, and driverless transportation.

I enjoy working with PLCs, control systems, and closed loop actuation systems, developing algorithms for smooth manipulation of robots.
